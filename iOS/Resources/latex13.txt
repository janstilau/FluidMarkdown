\section{Basic Mathematics}
\label{sec:basic-math}

Let's start with some basic mathematical expressions. The quadratic formula is given by:

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:quadratic}
x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}
\end{equation}

Equation~\ref{eq:quadratic} is fundamental in algebra and appears in Section~\ref{sec:basic-math} of Chapter~\ref{chap:math}.

\subsection{Linear Algebra}
\label{subsec:linear-algebra}

Matrix operations are essential in linear algebra. Consider the matrix equation:

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:matrix}
\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}
\end{equation}

where $\mathbf{A}$ is an $n \times n$ matrix, and $\mathbf{x}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ are $n$-dimensional vectors.

\subsubsection{Eigenvalues and Eigenvectors}
\label{subsubsec:eigenvalues}

For a square matrix $\mathbf{A}$, an eigenvalue $\lambda$ and corresponding eigenvector $\mathbf{v}$ satisfy:

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:eigenvalue}
\mathbf{A}\mathbf{v} = \lambda\mathbf{v}
\end{equation}

The relationship in Equation~\ref{eq:eigenvalue} is fundamental to many applications in physics and engineering.

\subsection{Calculus}
\label{subsec:calculus}

Calculus deals with rates of change and accumulation. The fundamental theorem of calculus states:

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ftc}
\int_a^b f'(x) dx = f(b) - f(a)
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Derivatives}
\label{subsubsec:derivatives}

The derivative of a function $f(x)$ is defined as:

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:derivative}
f'(x) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Integrals}
\label{subsubsec:integrals}

The definite integral is defined as:

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:integral}
\int_a^b f(x) dx = \lim_{n \to \infty} \sum_{i=1}^n f(x_i) \Delta x
\end{equation}

\section{Complex Analysis}
\label{sec:complex-analysis}

Complex analysis studies functions of complex variables. A complex number can be written as:

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:complex}
z = x + iy = r e^{i\theta}
\end{equation}

where $r = |z|$ and $\theta = \arg(z)$.

\subsection{Cauchy-Riemann Equations}
\label{subsec:cauchy-riemann}

For a function $f(z) = u(x,y) + iv(x,y)$ to be analytic, it must satisfy the Cauchy-Riemann equations:

\begin{align}
\label{eq:cr1}
\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} &= \frac{\partial v}{\partial y} \\
\label{eq:cr2}
\frac{\partial u}{\partial y} &= -\frac{\partial v}{\partial x}
\end{align}

Equations~\ref{eq:cr1} and~\ref{eq:cr2} are necessary conditions for analyticity.

\subsection{Residue Theorem}
\label{subsec:residue}

The residue theorem is a powerful tool for evaluating complex integrals:

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:residue}
\oint_C f(z) dz = 2\pi i \sum_{k} \text{Res}(f, z_k)
\end{equation}

where the sum is over all singularities $z_k$ inside the contour $C$.

\section{Differential Equations}
\label{sec:diff-eq}

Differential equations describe relationships between functions and their derivatives.

\subsection{Ordinary Differential Equations}
\label{subsec:ode}

A first-order linear ODE has the form:

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ode1}
\frac{dy}{dx} + P(x)y = Q(x)
\end{equation}

The solution involves an integrating factor $\mu(x) = e^{\int P(x) dx}$.

\subsection{Partial Differential Equations}
\label{subsec:pde}

The wave equation is a fundamental PDE:

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:wave}
\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial t^2} = c^2 \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2}
\end{equation}

Similarly, the heat equation is:

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:heat}
\frac{\partial u}{\partial t} = \alpha \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2}
\end{equation}
