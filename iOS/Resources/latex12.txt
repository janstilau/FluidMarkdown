\section{Multi-paragraph List Items}

Lists with detailed explanations:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Introduction to Algorithms}

    Algorithms are fundamental to computer science and programming. They provide step-by-step instructions for solving computational problems efficiently.
    
    Key characteristics include correctness, efficiency, and clarity. Good algorithms should produce correct results for all valid inputs.

\item \textbf{Data Structures}

    Data structures organize and store data in computer memory. Common examples include arrays, linked lists, stacks, queues, trees, and graphs.
    
    The choice of data structure significantly impacts the performance of algorithms that operate on the data.

\item \textbf{Complexity Analysis}

    Complexity analysis helps evaluate algorithm performance in terms of time and space requirements.
    
    Big O notation provides a mathematical framework for describing algorithmic complexity: $O(1), O(\log n), O(n), O(n \log n), O(n^2), O(2^n)$.

\end{enumerate}

\section{Theorem-like Lists}

Mathematical definitions and theorems:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Definition (Group):} A group $(G, \cdot)$ is a set $G$ with a binary operation $\cdot$ such that:
    \begin{itemize}
    \item Closure: $\forall a, b \in G, a \cdot b \in G$
    \item Associativity: $\forall a, b, c \in G, (a \cdot b) \cdot c = a \cdot (b \cdot c)$
    \item Identity: $\exists e \in G$ such that $\forall a \in G, e \cdot a = a \cdot e = a$
    \item Inverse: $\forall a \in G, \exists a^{-1} \in G$ such that $a \cdot a^{-1} = a^{-1} \cdot a = e$
    \end{itemize}

\item \textbf{Theorem (Fundamental Theorem of Calculus):} If $f$ is continuous on $[a,b]$, then:
    \begin{enumerate}
    \item $F(x) = \int_a^x f(t) dt$ is differentiable on $(a,b)$ and $F'(x) = f(x)$
    \item $\int_a^b f(x) dx = F(b) - F(a)$ where $F$ is any antiderivative of $f$
    \end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}
